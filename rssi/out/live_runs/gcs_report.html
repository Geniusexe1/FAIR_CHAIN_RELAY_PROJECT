The GCS layout works: 100% of UNIST with relay brains on their own NUCs

The big NUC runs as the GCS, NUC1 runs the chain robot and Gazebo, and NUC2 and NUC3 run one relay each. This is the first time relay brains on separate NUCs have explored a full map over the mesh without it collapsing.

Both runs used both relays, at P0 −45. The full-avoidance run also routed through both at once on 2 moves, the first time a live run did that in this setup.

What the comparison does and doesn't show

  • The avoidance settings did not cause the difference. In the full-avoidance run the guard and right of way never fired, so it behaved the same as the toned-down settings, apart from the guard being ready. The gap between 100% and 95.6% came from something else, most likely random variation in planning and the mesh conditions on the day.
  • Why I still recommend toned down. Across all runs so far, right of way has never fired, and the guard's only real effect was 25 aborts in a row in one run last night. The code adds nothing measurable. Removing it remains my recommendation, and it's your call. Note that the logged distances are samples about every 25 s, so they could miss a closer pass.
  • Each result is one run. Repeat runs would be needed before calling either setting better.
  • The toned-down run's last 4.4%: five goals were rejected about 1,000 times each, mostly as "skipped, nothing has moved since" after no plan could reach them with 2 relays. The goal-memory reset doesn't clear that skip list, so this trap is still open.

RTF

RTF improved to 0.41, against 0.27–0.28 with all brains on the big NUC and 0.31–0.35 in the older NUC1 and NUC2 runs. Gazebo here runs on NUC1, a slightly slower CPU than the big NUC, so the gain comes from moving the relay brains and coordinator off the simulating machine. There are also two relays now instead of three.

The loss sits on the wireless side of NUC1's radio, at about 6 Mbit/s, while NUC2 to NUC3 stayed mostly clean in the one check I ran. The cause isn't established. Candidates are the 15 or so nearby Wi-Fi networks, the radio's placement, the remaining multicast from NUC1, or a packet-rate limit in the radio. Exploration works through it, but plans do fail more often than they should, and a real deployment would feel this.

Other things to know

  • One unrealistic part: Gazebo on NUC1 means the relays' simulated scans cross the mesh. Real relays would have their own lidars.
  • False stop: my logger once froze for 8 minutes on a hung ssh and ended a healthy run as "FAST-LIO diverged". That run's data is in v3_false_stop/, and I restarted the test. The logger and the check are fixed.
  • Logger glitches: gz reads sometimes return another robot's pose, so I removed 42 and 27 impossible samples from these two runs' numbers. They can still appear as straight lines in the replay videos.
  • Coverage check: its reads over ssh sometimes failed and logged "0%" during the toned-down run. A long enough failure could look like a stall, though it didn't end a run. It should move to the GCS itself.
  • Machines: the nuc1 relay container and the big NUC's safe_robot and relays containers are stopped. The launcher edits the Nav2 launch file inside the NUC2 and NUC3 containers (TF and map remaps). The chain robot's own code and config files are unchanged; it now runs in NUC1's container with an environment setting for the mesh peers. The big NUC's firewall rule for 224.0.0.7 stays until a reboot.
  • Hospital: not run in this layout, as you asked.

What I'd do next

  1. Repeat each setting at least once, so the comparison stands on more than one run.
  2. Decide whether to remove the right-of-way and guard code.
  3. Let the goal-memory reset clear the skip list too, which targets the trap that held the toned-down run at 95.6%.
  4. When the loss matters again, try the multicast block on NUC1, then test the radio's placement and the flooding limit one at a time.
